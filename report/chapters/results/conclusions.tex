\chapter{Conclusions and future work}
\label{ch:res-conclusions}

\section{What was done}

This report set out to answer, empirically, which state estimator an electric-aircraft battery
management system should use, and to leave behind the means of asking the question again. The
answer required building the estimators: \estkit\ implements 70 cell-level estimators in ten
families, seven pack-level architectures and nine attitude estimators, each from its primary
reference, against one model-agnostic C++17 interface, without dynamic memory or exceptions,
in single or double precision, and cross-checked against FilterPy, PyBaMM and the \code{ahrs}
library where a reference exists. It required building the benchmark: two truth plants ---
an enhanced equivalent-circuit cell with thermal, hysteresis and ageing physics, and an
electrochemical single-particle model from published electrode data --- four missions, twelve
fault scenarios, a twelve-cell module, a 120-mission ageing study and a synthetic instrumented
flight, with metrics chosen for what a certification argument needs rather than for what is
convenient to compute. And it required writing it down: a chapter per estimator with the
derivation, the algorithm, the implementation and the measured behaviour, so that the reader
can see why each result is what it is.

\section{What was found}

Six findings carry the weight of the report.

On a cell whose model structure the estimator knows, the nominal problem is solved by any
correctly tuned closed-loop estimator, and the field is separated only by faults. Sensor faults
are handled by covariance adaptation or bounded influence functions at the cost of an EKF;
model faults are handled by estimators that keep their covariance honest --- fading memory,
consider parameters, statistically linearised or iterated updates, fixed gains --- and the
plain EKF fails the combined fault scenario not because it is a poor filter but because it is
an exact one for a model that is wrong.

On a cell whose model structure the estimator does not know, the ranking inverts. Every
converged Kalman-type filter parks on a \num{4}\,\% SOC bias created in the first high-current
transient, with a clean innovation sequence that gives no warning; the fading-memory EKF, the
Schmidt filter, the reduced-order filter and the fixed-gain observers hold \num{0.4}--\num{0.7}\,\%.
The voltage residual is not a health indicator under structured model error, and a BMS needs
a second estimate that does not share the model.

Capacity is identifiable only by an estimator with a capacity state, and then to the accuracy
of the current sensor's scale factor, across consecutive missions, provided the parameter
loop is gated on excitation. A current-sensor offset or gain error is not removable by any
estimator from voltage and current alone, at cell or at module level; it is a calibration
problem.

At module level the architecture matters more than the filter: averaging the voltage channels
inside the filter --- bar--delta or information fusion --- halves the per-cell error of
independent filters at a fraction of their cost, and finds the weak cell.

Cost does not discriminate among the estimators that matter. The best twelve of the composite
ranking run within five times the cost of the EKF, below \SI{2}{\micro\second} per step on a
desktop core and below a tenth of a percent of a flight processor per cell; the expensive
families --- Gauss--Hermite, particle, ensemble, moving-horizon --- buy no accuracy on this
problem that a cheaper estimator does not also deliver, with the exception of MHE's rejection
of a current-sensor bias. No estimator needs double precision.

On the aerospace heritage problem, the accelerometer is not a gravity reference for a third of a
flight that turns, and an estimator that knows this --- through a covariance model or a scalar
gate --- is an order of magnitude better than the published complementary filters that do not;
the gated Mahony filter matches the multiplicative EKF at a fifteenth of the cost, and the
residual two degrees of heading are set by the compass, not by the filter.

\section{What the library is for}

The estimators, the linear algebra and the model concept are independent of the battery. A
navigation model, a target-tracking model, an attitude model or an electrochemical model plugs
into every filter, observer, particle method and smoother in the library without modification
of the estimator code, as the attitude Part and the drone example of \cref{ch:library} show.
The battery-specific parts --- plants, datasets, scenarios, the cell-estimator adapter, the
registries --- are separate and are the template for the equivalent parts of another problem.
The intended use of the library after this report is as a common code base for state
estimation on embedded aerospace targets --- battery management, guidance and navigation of
uncrewed aircraft, attitude determination --- where the same requirements of determinism,
static memory, single precision and traceability to a published reference apply.

\section{Future work}

The benchmark suggests its own continuation.

\paragraph{An electrochemical estimator.} The single largest gap in the results is between the
equivalent-circuit and electrochemical plants, and the single most informative experiment not
yet run is the one that closes it: a reduced-order single-particle model --- a few diffusion
states per electrode, Butler--Volmer kinetics, an ohmic term --- as the \emph{estimator's}
model, run through the same filters on the same missions. The library's model concept admits it
without change; the identification tooling of \cref{ch:spm} produces its parameters; and the
result would say whether the \num{4}\,\% hover bias is removed at its source, as the physics
suggests, and at what cost in state dimension.

\paragraph{Measured data.} Every number in this report is simulated. The plants are built from
published electrode data and literature model structures, and the impedance, thermal, ageing
and sensor parameters are representative rather than measured. A characterisation of one
power cell --- OCV, pulse impedance over temperature, a few hundred cycles of ageing --- and
its replay through the same benchmark would turn the relative findings into absolute ones and
is the precondition for freezing any of the recommendations of \cref{ch:res-recommendations}.

\paragraph{Timing on the target.} The projections to a Cortex-M7 are estimates with stated
assumptions. Compiling the \code{float} build for an M7 and an M4F and measuring the
per-step cost of the reference stack, with the OCV table in flash, would replace a factor-of-two
uncertainty with a number.

\paragraph{Combined and gated estimators.} The reference architecture combines mechanisms
that were benchmarked separately: $\mR$ adaptation with bounded memory in one filter, an
excitation gate on the joint filter's parameter update, a disagreement flag between the filter
and the independent monitor. Each combination should be registered as an estimator and run
through the benchmark, with the same discipline that produced the results here.

\paragraph{Estimators not yet in the library.} The library is complete with respect to the
families of the literature but not with respect to their members. Candidates for the next
release include the cubature information filter and its square-root form, the ensemble
Kalman smoother, the Gaussian-process and Koopman-based data-driven filters, the
sum-of-norms and $\ell_1$-regularised moving-horizon formulations for fault-tolerant
estimation, an electrochemical PDE observer (backstepping on the diffusion equation), and the
distributed particle and consensus-ADMM methods for larger packs.

\section{Closing}

The most useful thing the benchmark says is also the simplest: the choice of estimator
matters much less than whether it is told the truth about its model and its sensors. A
\SI{0.5}{\micro\second} EKF that adapts its measurement noise and bounds its memory, with a
gated capacity state and an independent Coulomb counter beside it, on a calibrated current
sensor, has no failure scenario in this benchmark on either plant --- the two halves of that
recipe are the second-ranked estimator on the equivalent-circuit plant and the first on the
electrochemical one --- and it is the recommendation of this report. Everything else in the
library exists to show, on the same data, why.
