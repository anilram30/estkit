\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

State estimation is the core of a battery-management system (BMS): the quantities that
matter to an aircraft --- state of charge (SOC), state of health (SOH), remaining energy
and power --- are not measured but inferred from terminal voltage, current and temperature
through a model of the cell. This report documents \estkit, a header-only C++17 library of
state estimators and observers written against a single, model-agnostic interface, together
with a benchmark that exercises the library on the battery-management problem of an electric
aircraft. Every algorithm is implemented from its primary literature source, without dynamic
memory, exceptions or dependencies beyond the C++ standard library, so that the same code
compiles for a desktop benchmark and for a flight-controller class microcontroller in single
or double precision.

The cell-level benchmark comprises 70 SOC estimators in ten families: Coulomb-counting and
OCV baselines; classical and sigma-point Kalman filters (EKF, iterated and second-order EKF,
UKF, cubature and Gauss--Hermite filters, iterated posterior linearisation, information,
square-root and U-D forms); adaptive filters (Sage--Husa, innovation-based, variational
Bayesian, strong-tracking, fading-memory, fuzzy, IMM and MMAE); robust and embedded filters
(Schmidt--Kalman, $H_\infty$, Huber, maximum-correntropy, Student's~$t$, constrained,
reduced-order, SVSF); deterministic observers (Luenberger, PI, sliding-mode and
super-twisting, high-gain, extended-state, unknown-input, disturbance, adaptive and interval
observers); joint and dual state--parameter filters for SOH (joint/dual EKF and UKF, RLS
identification, approximate weighted total least squares); particle, ensemble and
Gaussian-sum filters (bootstrap, fast, regularised, auxiliary, Rao--Blackwellised and
unscented particle filters, EnKF, ETKF, GSF); data-driven hybrids (direct neural regression,
NN--EKF, ELM--RLS--EKF); moving-horizon estimation in real-time-iteration and converged form;
and offline smoothers (extended and unscented Rauch--Tung--Striebel, fixed-lag, batch
nonlinear least squares). Pack-level estimation (decentralised, bar-delta, federated,
consensus and information-fusion filters on a twelve-cell module), a multi-flight
capacity-tracking study, and an attitude-estimation benchmark (complementary, Madgwick,
Mahony, multiplicative and invariant EKF, TRIAD/QUEST) complete the picture.

Two truth plants generate the data: an enhanced equivalent-circuit cell with lumped thermal
dynamics, Arrhenius impedance, one-state and Preisach hysteresis and cycle/calendar ageing,
and an electrochemical single-particle model built from the published LG~M50 electrode data
and validated against PyBaMM. Twelve fault and operating scenarios (initial-SOC error,
current-sensor bias and gain error, noise steps, outliers, cold and hot operation, ageing,
parameter mismatch, sensor dropout, Preisach hysteresis and a combined worst case) are run on
eVTOL, fixed-wing, hover and ground-charging missions with three noise realisations each.
The Kalman-type implementations are cross-checked against FilterPy and the attitude filters
against the \texttt{ahrs} library to floating-point precision.

Every estimator is documented in its own chapter --- derivation with every symbol defined,
algorithm box, implementation notes, cost, and measured behaviour --- and the closing part
synthesises the close to nine thousand benchmark runs into recommendations for an
electric-aircraft BMS.
